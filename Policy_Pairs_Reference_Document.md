# Service Agent Research Study
## Policy Pairs Reference Document

**10 Service Domains × 2 Scenarios Each = 20 Topics**

*Utilitarian (Functional) vs. Hedonic (Experiential) Impact*

---

# Study Overview

This document describes the 10 policy pairs (20 total topics) used in the Service Agent Research Study. Each pair consists of two scenarios within the same service domain:

- **Utilitarian Scenario (U):** Focuses on functional/practical loss—the customer cannot achieve their intended goal
- **Hedonic Scenario (H):** Focuses on experiential/emotional loss—the customer's enjoyment or trust is damaged

**Key Distinction:** In utilitarian scenarios, the product/service fundamentally fails to perform. In hedonic scenarios, functionality may be intact but the experience is diminished.

# Pair 1: Food Delivery 🍔

## Policy Overview

**Policy:** Handling Missing or Poorly Presented Items in Food Delivery

**Scope:** Applicable to online chat interactions when a customer reports one or more missing items, and/or messy or unappetizing presentation of delivered food.

**Goal:** Ensure consistent, efficient, and fair resolution for common food delivery issues, while allowing flexibility in conversational tone.

### Agent Workflow

1. Identify the customer's issue type: Missing item(s), Messy presentation, or Both
2. Request only essential information: Order reference, Affected item(s), Description of the issue
3. Categorize severity: Low (non-essential missing/cosmetic), Medium (side/add-on or messy but edible), High (entrée missing/inedible), Safety Concern (escalate immediately)
4. Select resolution: Refund, Account credit, Redelivery, or Escalation based on severity
5. Execute, confirm, and document the interaction

### Escalation Triggers

- Tampering, broken seals, or foreign objects reported
- Allergens missing or incorrect substitutions
- Food reported as undercooked, spoiled, or causing illness
- Repeated complaint from customer
- Customer requests action beyond authorization

---

## Scenario U1: Missing Food Item

**Type:** Utilitarian (Functional Loss)

**Description:** The customer ordered dinner for their family. When the order arrived, the main entrée—a large chicken shawarma platter meant to feed three people—was completely missing from the bag. The sides and drinks were there, but without the main dish, no one has enough to eat.

**Impact:** Functional: Cannot fulfill the basic purpose of the order (feeding the family). Creates immediate practical problem.

### Customer Stimulus (Shown to Participant)

> You ordered dinner for yourself and your family through a food delivery app. When the order arrived, you noticed that the main entrée—a large chicken shawarma platter that was meant to feed three people—was completely missing from the bag. The sides and drinks were there, but without the main dish, no one has enough to eat. You're hungry, your family is waiting, and you need this resolved.

### Initial Customer Message

> "Hi, I just received my food delivery order and the main dish is completely missing. My order number is #FD-847291. The chicken shawarma platter that was supposed to feed my whole family isn't in the bag. We're all hungry and waiting. Can you help?"

**Resolution Focus:** Prioritize getting food to the customer quickly—either through redelivery of the missing item or full refund so they can order elsewhere. Speed matters.

**Type:** Hedonic (Experiential Loss)

**Description:** The customer ordered a special meal to celebrate a personal milestone. When they opened the containers, the food was a complete mess—sauces had spilled everywhere, the presentation was ruined, toppings were scattered, and containers were crushed. Everything is technically edible, but the experience is ruined.

> You ordered a special meal from your favorite restaurant through a delivery app to celebrate a small personal milestone. When you opened the containers, the food was a complete mess—sauces had spilled everywhere, the presentation was ruined, toppings were scattered, and containers were crushed. Everything is technically edible, but the experience you were looking forward to is completely spoiled.

> "Hi, I just received my food order and it's a total mess. I ordered this for a special occasion, but everything is spilled and crushed. The containers leaked everywhere and the food looks terrible. Order #FD-923156. I know it's probably still edible but this really ruined what was supposed to be a nice moment. Can you help?"

# Pair 2: Ride-Hailing 🚗

## Policy Overview

**Policy:** Handling Ride-Hailing Issues – Route Inefficiency vs. Uncomfortable Experience

**Scope:** Applies to online chat interactions when a customer reports that a ride-hailing trip resulted in delay or inefficiency due to poor route choice, and/or discomfort or stress due to the driver's behavior.

**Goal:** Classify experience and timing complaints, apply appropriate resolutions, and maintain rider confidence in platform safety and reliability.

### Agent Workflow

1. Determine whether the rider's complaint is about Functional loss (inefficient route causing delay) or Experiential loss (uncomfortable interaction regardless of timing)
2. Collect basic details: Trip ID, arrival time impact, description of concern, safety feelings
3. Classify severity: Low (slight detour/awkwardness), Medium (5-15 min delay or clear discomfort), High (significant delay or distressing behavior), Critical (safety concerns—escalate)
4. Offer resolution: Partial/full fare refund, account credit, or escalation for follow-up
5. Execute, confirm, and document the case

### Escalation Triggers

- Threatening, discriminatory, or aggressive behavior from driver
- Rider feeling unsafe or requesting formal follow-up
- Harassment, physical contact, or intoxicated driving
- Repeated issues with same driver or pattern of complaints
- Delay resulting in missed high-stakes appointment (flight, interview)

---

## Scenario U2: Inefficient Route Delay

**Type:** Utilitarian (Functional Loss)

**Description:** The customer booked a ride to get to an important job interview on time. The driver took an unnecessarily long route, ignoring GPS suggestions. What should have been a 20-minute ride took 40 minutes, and the customer arrived 10 minutes late.

**Impact:** Functional: Time wasted, potential job opportunity affected. Concrete, measurable harm to the customer's goals.

### Customer Stimulus (Shown to Participant)

> You booked a ride to get to an important job interview on time. The driver took an unnecessarily long route, ignoring the GPS suggestions and going through side streets. What should have been a 20-minute ride took 40 minutes, and you arrived 10 minutes late to your interview. You're upset about the wasted time and the potential impact on your opportunity.

### Initial Customer Message

> "Hi, I need to report a problem with my ride. Trip ID #RH-55829. I had an important job interview and the driver took a completely inefficient route. A 20-minute trip took 40 minutes and I was late to my interview. The driver ignored the GPS and went through random side streets. This may have cost me a job opportunity. What can you do about this?"

**Resolution Focus:** Acknowledge the concrete harm (lateness, potential job impact). Offer fare refund and consider additional credit given the high stakes.

---

## Scenario H2: Uncomfortable Ride Experience

**Type:** Hedonic (Experiential Loss)

**Description:** The customer took a ride to meet friends for dinner. The driver was playing loud music, made several personal phone calls, and the car had an unpleasant smell. The customer arrived on time, but the entire ride was uncomfortable and stressful.

**Impact:** Experiential: No functional failure (arrived on time) but the experience was unpleasant. Emotional stress before a social event.

### Customer Stimulus (Shown to Participant)

> You took a ride to meet friends for dinner. The driver was playing loud music you didn't enjoy, made several personal phone calls during the trip, and the car had an unpleasant smell. You arrived on time, but the entire ride was uncomfortable and stressful. What should have been a relaxing trip left you feeling tense and annoyed before your evening out.

### Initial Customer Message

> "Hi, I want to report a very uncomfortable ride experience. Trip ID #RH-67234. The driver had loud music blasting, made personal calls the whole time, and the car smelled bad. I got to my destination on time but the whole experience was really unpleasant and stressful. I was meeting friends and arrived feeling tense instead of relaxed. Is there anything you can do?"

**Resolution Focus:** Validate the discomfort even though arrival was on time. Offer credit or partial refund acknowledging the poor experience.

# Pair 3: Airline ✈️

## Policy Overview

**Policy:** Handling Airline Meal Service Issues – Dietary Requirement vs. Flight Enjoyment

**Scope:** Applies to online chat interactions when a passenger reports not receiving a pre-purchased or requested in-flight meal, including dietary/medical meal requirements not being met and pre-selected or paid meals not served as expected.

**Goal:** Classify complaints by functional vs. experiential impact, resolve fairly, and ensure systemic logging for catering and fulfillment review.

### Agent Workflow

1. Establish whether the missing meal was intended to satisfy a medical/dietary requirement (Functional) or enhance the in-flight experience (Experiential)
2. Gather details: Flight number/date, Meal type ordered, Whether meal was paid or loyalty-program, Alternative food access during flight
3. Classify severity: Low (minor disappointment, alternatives available), Medium (paid meal not served, no medical concern), High (dietary/medical restriction unmet), Critical (illness, hypoglycemia, extended fasting—escalate)
4. Offer resolution: Full/partial refund, travel credit, loyalty points, or escalation
5. Execute, confirm, and document

### Escalation Triggers

- Passenger reports illness or significant health impact
- Complaint involves medical, allergy-sensitive, or religious dietary restrictions unmet
- Pattern of repeated non-fulfillment on same route/provider
- Legal or regulatory request tied to airline service
- Customer demands formal documentation or follow-up directly from airline

---

## Scenario U3: Dietary Meal Not Provided

**Type:** Utilitarian (Functional Loss)

**Description:** The customer pre-ordered a vegetarian meal for an 8-hour international flight because they have dietary restrictions. During meal service, there was no record of the request and no vegetarian options remained. The customer had to go hungry for the entire flight.

**Impact:** Functional: Could not eat due to dietary restrictions. Physical discomfort and health implications over a long flight.

### Customer Stimulus (Shown to Participant)

> You pre-ordered a special vegetarian meal for your 8-hour international flight because you have dietary restrictions. When meal service came, the flight attendants said they had no record of your request and all vegetarian options were gone. You couldn't eat the regular meals due to your dietary needs and had to go hungry for the remainder of a very long flight.

### Initial Customer Message

> "Hi, I need help with a serious issue from my flight. Flight #AL-892, seat 24C. I pre-ordered a vegetarian meal because I have dietary restrictions, but when meal service came, they said there was no record of it and no vegetarian options left. I couldn't eat anything on an 8-hour flight. I went hungry the entire time. This was really difficult. Can you help?"

**Resolution Focus:** Take seriously—this is a failure with physical consequences. Full meal refund plus additional compensation for the discomfort. Consider escalation to ensure it doesn't recur.

When the meal arrived, it was cold, the portions were smaller than advertised, and the quality was far below what was shown in the menu photos. I could eat other food, but I paid for something special and didn't get it at all. This really took away from my flight experience. What can be done?"

**Resolution Focus:** Refund the premium meal upgrade cost. Offer additional credit or miles as goodwill for the disappointing experience.

# Pair 4: Hotel 🏨

## Policy Overview

**Policy:** Handling Hotel Check-in Issues – Room Not Ready (Functional vs. Experiential Impact)

**Scope:** Applies to online chat interactions when a hotel guest reports that their room was not ready at the expected check-in time, including practical disruption of plans and emotional disappointment due to delay.

**Goal:** Categorize guest impact as functional or experiential, resolve the disruption fairly, and document for operational accountability.

### Agent Workflow

1. Determine whether the guest experienced Functional loss (delay interfered with rest, work, or preparation) or Experiential loss (felt unwelcome or disappointed)
2. Collect context: Scheduled vs. actual check-in time, Duration of delay, Whether alternatives were offered, Guest plans impacted
3. Classify severity: Low (<30 min, no disruption), Medium (30-90 min, some inconvenience), High (90+ min or missed key activity), Critical (formal event, medical needs—escalate)
4. Offer resolution: Room upgrade, Fee waiver, Partial refund, Hotel credit, Loyalty points, or Escalation
5. Execute, confirm, and document

### Escalation Triggers

- Guest missed a formal event or work obligation due to delay
- Guest has accessibility, health, or childcare needs unmet by delay
- Guest expresses emotional distress or demands formal follow-up
- Guest is VIP or loyalty-tier member with elevated expectations
- Multiple delays across same property or chain

---

**Description:** The customer arrived at the hotel for a business trip after a long flight, needing to prepare for an important presentation. Despite booking early check-in, the room wasn't ready for over 2 hours. They couldn't access luggage to change or set up their laptop to work.

Despite booking an early check-in, your room wasn't ready for over 2 hours. You couldn't access your luggage to change or set up your laptop to work. The delay significantly cut into your preparation time for a critical business meeting.

> "Hi, I had a really frustrating experience at check-in. Reservation #HT-29384. I booked and paid for early check-in because I had an important business presentation to prepare for. I arrived after a long flight, but my room wasn't ready for over 2 hours. I couldn't change clothes, couldn't work on my laptop, lost critical preparation time. This was a business trip and the delay really impacted my work. What can you do?"

**Resolution Focus:** Acknowledge the professional impact. Refund early check-in fee, offer credit toward future stay, and consider upgrade for current or future booking.

# Pair 5: Retail 📦

## Policy Overview

**Policy:** Handling Damaged Product Deliveries – Utility vs. Enjoyment Impact

**Scope:** Applies to online chat interactions when a customer reports receiving a damaged product from an online retail order, including products unusable due to damage and products still usable but with diminished unboxing or gifting experience.

**Goal:** Classify the impact of damage as functional or experiential, determine refund or replacement eligibility, and ensure proper documentation for fulfillment review.

### Agent Workflow

1. Confirm whether the item is unusable for its intended purpose (Functional loss) or usable but with appearance/experience affected (Experiential loss)
2. Request details: Order number, Item name, Description of damage, Optional photo, Whether item was for gifting/display/special use
3. Classify severity: Low (minor cosmetic, functions fine), Medium (noticeable damage, partially usable), High (unusable or unsafe), Special (gift or collectible with appearance-based value)
4. Offer resolution: Full refund, Free replacement, Partial refund, Store credit, or Escalation
5. Execute, confirm, and document

### Escalation Triggers

- Customer received multiple damaged items in one order
- Item is high-value or limited edition
- Customer indicates emotional distress (missed gifting moment)
- Safety concern (exposed wires, leaking contents)
- Package tampering suspected or delivery partner misconduct reported

---

# Pair 6: Subscription 💳

## Policy Overview

**Policy:** Handling Incorrect Subscription Charges – Financial vs. Emotional Impact

**Scope:** Applies to online chat interactions when a customer reports being incorrectly charged for a subscription service, including billing errors creating financial burden and billing issues causing frustration, distrust, or negative brand sentiment.

**Goal:** Classify issue by functional vs. emotional impact, resolve promptly and fairly, and ensure billing systems are updated accordingly.

### Agent Workflow

1. Determine whether the charge created Functional loss (financial disruption, need to contact banks) or Emotional loss (frustration, mistrust, brand dissatisfaction)
2. Confirm details: Account ID/email, Date and amount of charge, Whether customer canceled/paused but was still charged, Whether refund already issued/requested
3. Classify severity: Low (duplicate resolved quickly), Medium (led to overdraft/fees or unexpected renewal), High (customer disputed with bank or financial stress), Emotional (loss of trust, loyalty concerns)
4. Offer resolution: Full refund, Fee reimbursement, Account credit, Escalation to billing team, Feedback to internal teams
5. Execute, confirm, and document

### Escalation Triggers

- Customer reports overdraft, bank dispute, or financial penalty
- Refund not visible after promised timeframe
- Repeated incorrect charges across multiple cycles
- System error suspected (canceled account still charged)
- Customer requests formal investigation or legal documentation

---

**Type:** Utilitarian (Functional Loss)

**Description:** The customer canceled their subscription two months ago but was charged for both months since. The unexpected charges caused an overdraft, resulting in additional bank fees. They now have to spend time disputing charges and dealing with their bank.

**Impact:** Functional: Concrete financial harm—overdraft fees, time spent resolving. Cascading practical consequences.

### Customer Stimulus (Shown to Participant)

> You canceled your subscription service two months ago, but you just noticed you've been charged for both months since then. The unexpected charges caused your account to overdraft, resulting in additional bank fees. You now have to spend time disputing charges and dealing with your bank, all because of billing that should have stopped.

### Initial Customer Message

> "Hi, I have a serious billing issue. Account #SB-48291. I canceled my subscription two months ago but I just saw I've been charged for both months anyway. These charges caused my bank account to overdraft and I got hit with fees. I need these charges reversed and I need help with the overdraft fees this caused. I did everything right to cancel and now I'm dealing with this mess. Please help."

**Resolution Focus:** Immediate full refund of both charges. Reimburse overdraft fees caused by the error. Provide documentation if needed for bank dispute.

# Pair 7: Telecom 📡

## Policy Overview

**Policy:** Handling Internet Outage Complaints – Work Disruption vs. Downtime Frustration

**Scope:** Applies to online chat interactions when a customer reports internet service was unavailable for an extended period, including service interruptions affecting work/school and interruptions affecting personal time or relaxation plans.

**Goal:** Classify outage impact, provide resolution based on functional or experiential loss, and ensure accurate outage logging.

### Agent Workflow

1. Determine whether the outage disrupted work/school tasks (Functional loss) or personal downtime (Experiential loss)
2. Ask: Duration and time range of outage, Impact on work/school vs. personal time, Previous troubleshooting/contact attempts, Whether issue is resolved or ongoing
3. Classify severity: Low (<1 hour, no major disruption), Medium (1-3 hours, affected streaming/browsing), High (3+ hours or missed obligations), Critical (urgent deadlines, work penalty—escalate)
4. Offer resolution: Pro-rated credit, Flat goodwill credit, Network team escalation, Service extension, Follow-up
5. Execute, confirm, and document

### Escalation Triggers

- Customer reports repeated outages within short timeframe
- Customer requests formal complaint or service downgrade
- Area shows multiple complaints and system outage not registered
- Customer suffered financial or academic penalty due to outage
- Safety concerns related to smart home or medical equipment affected

# Pair 8: Events 🎫

## Policy Overview

**Policy:** Handling Event Cancellations – Schedule Disruption vs. Disappointment

**Scope:** Applies to online chat interactions when a customer reports that a scheduled event they had tickets for was canceled, including practical disruption to schedule/travel plans and emotional disappointment due to anticipation or significance.

**Goal:** Distinguish functional from experiential impacts, process applicable refunds or credits, and maintain goodwill where events are uncontrollable.

1. Determine whether the cancellation disrupted plans (Functional) or created emotional disappointment (Experiential)
2. Gather information: Event name/date, Ticket reference, Whether travel/accommodation was affected, Whether this was a special occasion, Refund status, Interest in alternatives

### Escalation Triggers

- Customer incurred financial loss beyond ticket cost
- Cancellation affected special event (proposal, birthday, graduation)
- Refund not processed within published timeframe
- Customer requests legal/press escalation or formal review
- Discrepancy between partner/organizer policy and customer experience

# Pair 9: Banking 🏦

## Policy Overview

**Policy:** Handling Banking Transaction Errors – Practical Disruption vs. Financial Anxiety

**Scope:** Applies to online chat interactions when a customer reports an error with a bank transaction, including failed/misprocessed payments creating practical issues and emotional distress caused by perceived instability in account activity.

**Goal:** Distinguish between functional and emotional impact, resolve verified errors swiftly, and ensure secure handling of financial concerns.

### Agent Workflow

1. Determine whether the concern is outcome-based (payment failure, fees) or perception-based (anxiety, trust loss)
2. Confirm details: Date and type of transaction, Amount and recipient, Whether error led to failed payment/duplicate/delay, Downstream effects (fees, penalties), Emotional state
3. Classify severity: Low (reversible error, funds intact), Medium (missed deadline or inconvenience), High (fees, bounced payment, reputational harm), Emotional (anxiety, distrust, fear)
4. Offer resolution: Immediate reversal/refund, Fee reimbursement, Account credit, Escalation for audit, Case reference for verification
5. Execute, confirm, and document

### Escalation Triggers

- Customer reports repeated transaction errors
- Affected payment involves legal, tax, or loan obligation
- Customer expresses loss of access to funds or livelihood
- Potential fraud or unauthorized access suspected
- Customer requests formal investigation or documentation for third parties

# Pair 10: E-commerce 🛒

## Policy Overview

**Policy:** Handling Incorrect E-commerce Deliveries – Utility vs. Trust Impact

**Scope:** Applies to online chat interactions when a customer reports receiving the wrong item in an e-commerce order, including incorrect items preventing planned use and incorrect items that reduce trust or satisfaction in the shopping experience.

**Goal:** Identify whether the impact is functional or emotional, resolve quickly with proper logistics, and capture fulfillment feedback.

### Agent Workflow

1. Determine whether the wrong item impacted usage plans (Functional) or confidence/trust in the brand (Experiential)
2. Request details: Order number, Intended item name/variant, Description or photo of received item, Whether item is usable for original purpose, Whether issue impacted trust or special plans
3. Classify severity: Low (minor mismatch, still usable), Medium (different product, not usable for intended purpose), High (urgent use disrupted or repeated incidents), Experiential (disappointment, trust loss)
4. Offer resolution: Reship correct item, Full refund, Partial refund/credit if keeping incorrect item, Store credit/goodwill, Escalation for repeat issues
5. Execute, confirm, and document

### Escalation Triggers

- Customer received wrong item more than once
- Wrong item delivered in high-value or urgent order
- Customer is loyalty/VIP tier member
- Issue involves fraud risk, tampering, or shipping partner error
- Customer demands formal follow-up or legal documentation
